# L8R Editorial v2: full collection review

Reanalyzed on 2026-09-03 from all 40 supplied images. Thirty-five are actual 257-269px thumbnails despite the larger dimensions in their filenames. Composition and palette observations use all 40; texture and conditioning decisions use the five genuinely larger originals. No new AI artwork was generated for this analysis.

## The substance, not just the surface

The strongest editorial images combine a recognizable subject with a deliberate graphic intervention. The photograph carries identity and factual detail; flat color, a cutout, a scale change or an unexpected juxtaposition carries the editorial point. The result is a designed magazine photomontage, not a decorative simulation of "advanced technology."

The recurring grammar is:

- Photographic cutouts, sometimes monochrome or duotone, retain an identifiable silhouette.
- Flat, saturated color fields separate layers and direct attention. Cobalt/ultramarine is a frequent anchor; orange, yellow, coral and magenta are accents, not a requirement to use every color.
- Print grain, stipple and selective halftone unify otherwise different materials. This is matte photographic/printed texture, not shiny plastic or uniformly blurred noise.
- One subject/idea relationship leads. Repetition, exaggerated scale and paper shapes are useful when they explain the news; they are not decorations to apply mechanically.
- Negative space and readable silhouettes matter at newsletter size. A complex source photo can be simplified by duotone or masking without erasing what the story is about.

The collection also contains glowing systems, blurred data corridors and digitally rendered objects. Those are secondary treatments. Promoting them to the default would recreate the generic output this change is meant to prevent. We retain their lessons about contrast and hierarchy, not their glow/CGI vocabulary.

## The three conditioning images, in fixed order

1. **Energy landscape — `aa9fc889...`, 1024x512.** Primary anchor for photographic collage, blue/yellow duotone, flat vermilion fields and layered depth. Best broad representation of the intended magazine language. Do not copy its power plant, pylons, mountains or sun.
2. **Lobster and paper hand — `d12fdb3d...`, 1456x816.** Anchor for an oversized recognizable focal subject, strong silhouette, cobalt/magenta/yellow separation and visible print texture. Do not copy its lobster, hand or network lines.
3. **Cloud collage — `bdc42d57...`, 1456x816.** Anchor for restraint in abstract stories: photographic material, black negative space, one warm accent and visibly flat layers. Do not copy the cloud, Apple logo, circle or exact layout.

The other two large originals are not conditioning defaults: the **coin field (`8072db31...`)** is useful for repetition but risks making unrelated stories into generic finance renders; the **glowing globe (`845dd4f2...`)** emphasizes digital light effects that conflict with the chosen print-collage baseline. The 35 thumbnails remain analysis material only. They are not upscaled into conditioning anchors.

## Execution contract

The 40 images inform this local analysis once. Only the selected three are uploaded to Supabase, together with the extracted style rules. The other 37 remain local analysis material. The selected pack stores exactly three asset/conditioning IDs in the order above. A render request contains the compiled scene/style prompt, those three real image inputs, and the selected news image inputs; subject uploads or an edit source appear only when the user explicitly supplies them. The collection is never uploaded wholesale or sent wholesale to renderers.

The planner must separate **what the story is about** from **how L8R depicts it**. News images supply factual appearance, not website/advertising art direction. Missing default style is an error before paid planning/rendering; it is not the same as an explicit None choice. A changed prompt version invalidates old machine-generated plans without deleting outputs or user-written prompts.

The canonical executable profile, three reference lessons and planner system instruction live together in `src/lib/studio/editorial-style.ts`. `prompts.ts` compiles that profile into the renderer request and gives the quality checker the independent expected style, not only the generated scene plan. Real output quality still needs human review; no new sample was generated during this repair.
